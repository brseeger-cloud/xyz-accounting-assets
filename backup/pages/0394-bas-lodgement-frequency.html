BAS lodgement: monthly, quarterly or annually?

Most small businesses lodge their BAS quarterly. You must report GST monthly once your GST turnover reaches $20 million. You can also choose monthly if you’re regularly owed GST refunds. Some businesses that register for GST voluntarily can report GST just once a year.

Updated 16 September 2026 Current for 2026-27

What sets your BAS frequency

A business activity statement can report several obligations at once: GST, PAYG withholding from wages, and PAYG instalments towards your income tax. Each has its own frequency rules. Your BAS cycle follows whichever obligation has to be reported most often.

GST: monthly or quarterly

GST turnover How often you report GST
$20 million or more Monthly, compulsory
Under $20 million Quarterly by default, or monthly if you choose
Voluntarily registered, under the GST registration threshold Quarterly, or annually if you elect it

When monthly makes sense. If you usually claim more GST back than you collect, you’re waiting up to three months for money the ATO owes you. Exporters, businesses in a heavy set-up phase and anyone buying big equipment often fall into this group. Reporting monthly brings those refunds forward. The cost is twelve lodgements a year instead of four. Once you elect monthly reporting, you generally have to stay on it for at least 12 months.

When annual makes sense. If you registered for GST voluntarily and your turnover is below the registration threshold, annual GST reporting removes most of the paperwork. You still report PAYG withholding at its own frequency if you have employees.

PAYG withholding from wages

  • Small withholders ($25,000 or less withheld a year): report and pay quarterly.
  • Medium withholders ($25,001 to $1 million): report and pay monthly.
  • Large withholders (more than $1 million): pay electronically within a few days of each pay run.

So a business that reports GST quarterly but has grown into a medium withholder will lodge a BAS every month, and GST is only reported on the quarterly ones.

Due dates

Quarter Due if you lodge yourself
July – September 28 October
October – December 28 February
January – March 28 April
April – June 28 July

A monthly BAS is generally due on the 21st of the following month. When a registered agent lodges quarterly BAS electronically, the ATO usually allows extra time beyond the dates above. The exact date depends on the quarter and your lodgement history, so confirm it with your agent rather than assuming.

Changing your frequency

You can ask to change your GST reporting cycle through ATO online services, the Business Portal or your registered agent. Changes normally start from the beginning of the next reporting period, not partway through one. When your turnover or wages cross a threshold, the ATO will usually move you to the new cycle itself and tell you in writing. Read those letters carefully, because a missed monthly BAS attracts the same penalties as a missed quarterly one.
