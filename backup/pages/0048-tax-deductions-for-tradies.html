Tax deductions for tradies and construction workers

Tradies and construction workers can claim tools and equipment, protective clothing and boots, vehicle: travel between sites, licences, tickets and white card renewals — provided you paid for them yourself, they relate to earning your income, and you kept a record.

Updated 2026-08-21 · Current for 2026–27

Tools, protective gear and travel between sites make up most tradie claims — and vehicle expenses are where the ATO looks hardest, so the logbook matters.

What you can claim

Tools and equipment
$900 typical

Under $300 each is an immediate deduction. Over that, claim the decline in value.

Protective clothing and boots
$400 typical

Hi-vis, steel caps, hard hats, sun protection for outdoor work.

Vehicle: travel between sites
$1,500 typical

Site to site is deductible; home to your first site usually is not. 88c per km up to 5,000 km, or a logbook.

Licences, tickets and white card renewals
$250 typical

Renewals of tickets you need for the job you already have.

Working from home
$700 typical

The fixed rate covers energy, internet, phone and stationery. You need a record of the hours.

Last year’s accountant fee
$99 typical

The cost of managing your tax affairs is deductible in the year you pay it.

“Typical” is what we commonly see across returns in this occupation, not an entitlement. You can only claim what you actually spent, and you need to be able to show it.

What tradies and construction workers most often get wrong

  • Carrying bulky tools does not automatically make the trip from home deductible — the ATO tightened this.
  • Tools you also use at home need apportioning to the work-use share.
  • A ute is not automatically 100% work use.

The $1,000 floor from 2026-27

From 1 July 2026 the standard deduction gives every taxpayer $1,000 of work-related expenses with no receipts. You take the higher of that or your itemised claim.

Typical claims in this occupation come to around $3,849, comfortably above the floor — so keep your records and claim the real figure.

What it is worth

A deduction reduces the income you are taxed on, so it comes back at your marginal rate plus the Medicare levy. At the 30% bracket, $1,000 of claims is worth $320 — not $1,000. See what you can claim for the general rules, or put your own figures in below.

Questions people actually ask

What can tradies and construction workers claim on tax?
tools and equipment, protective clothing and boots, vehicle: travel between sites, licences, tickets and white card renewals, working from home, last year’s accountant fee — provided you paid for them yourself, they relate to earning your income, and you have a record. Typical claims across these categories come to around $3,849, but you can only claim what you actually spent.

Do I need receipts for all of it?
If your total work-related claims come to more than $300 you need written evidence for all of them, not just the amount over $300. Below $300 you still need to be able to explain how you worked out the figure.

Is it worth using an agent as a tradie?
It generally pays for itself where your occupation has claims that are easy to miss or easy to get wrong — and the agent fee is itself deductible next year. If your return is one job and no expenses, self-lodging is perfectly reasonable and we would rather say so.
