How withholding actually works

Your employer does not know what you will earn for the year. So the tables take the pay in front of them, annualise it, work out the tax on that annual figure, and divide it back down. It is a reasonable guess that assumes your income is identical every pay and that you have no deductions at all.

That assumption is why most people get a refund. It is also why a bonus feels over-taxed: the tables briefly treat you as though you earn a bonus every fortnight.

The two settings that cause almost every problem

The tax-free threshold. Claim it at one job only, the highest-paying one. With two jobs both claiming it, each withholds as if you had no other income and you finish the year short.

The study loan flag. If you have a HECS-HELP debt and did not tick the box on your TFN declaration, nothing is withheld towards it all year and the whole compulsory repayment lands as a bill at assessment. Toggle it above to see the size of the difference.

If the numbers do not match your payslip

Check the TFN declaration settings with your payroll team first — they are almost always the cause. Salary sacrifice, novated leases and reportable fringe benefits also change the taxable figure your withholding is based on, and none of those appear in this calculator. If you are still out by a meaningful amount, that is worth a conversation before 30 June rather than after.

Questions people actually ask

How much tax should be taken out of my weekly pay?
It depends on the amount, whether you claim the tax-free threshold at that job, and whether you have a study loan. Enter your gross pay above and the calculator shows the withholding and the net figure that should hit your account.

Why is my employer withholding a different amount?
Employers use the ATO withholding schedules, which work in whole-dollar steps and round each pay. A difference of a dollar or two per pay is normal and squares up when you lodge. A difference of tens of dollars usually means a tax-free threshold or study loan setting is wrong on your TFN declaration.

Should I claim the tax-free threshold at more than one job?
No. Claim it at the job that pays you the most. Claiming it twice means both employers withhold as though the other job does not exist, and you end up owing money at the end of the year. This is the single most common cause of an unexpected tax bill.

What if I am paid irregularly?
Withholding tables annualise each individual pay, so an unusually large pay is withheld at a higher rate than your real annual position warrants. It comes back at lodgement. Bonuses and back pay have their own schedules.
