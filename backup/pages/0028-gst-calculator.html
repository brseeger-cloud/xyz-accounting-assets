Why it is one eleventh, not ten per cent

GST is 10% of the pre-GST price, so a $100 item becomes $110. Working backwards from $110, the GST is $10 — which is one eleventh of $110, not 10% of it. Taking 10% off the inclusive figure gives $11, and that error compounds across a whole BAS.

Getting the BAS figures right

Your BAS wants GST-exclusive amounts at G1 and the GST itself at 1A and 1B. Most accounting software handles the split, but manual entries and cash-basis reconciliations are where the one eleventh rule earns its keep.

A worked example. You invoice $5,500 including GST. The GST is $500 — one eleventh — and your income is $5,000. Take 10% off the $5,500 instead and you would report $550 of GST and $4,950 of income, overstating the GST payable and understating your income at the same time.

The GST you collect is not your money

From the moment a customer pays you, one eleventh of that payment belongs to the ATO. The businesses that get into trouble with GST are almost never the ones that miscalculated it — they are the ones that spent it. Move it out the day it arrives.

Tax invoices

For a purchase over $82.50 including GST you need a valid tax invoice to claim the credit: the supplier’s identity and ABN, the date, a description, the GST amount and the words “tax invoice”. A bank statement line is not enough, and this is the first thing checked in a GST review.

Questions people actually ask

How do I work out the GST in a price?
Divide the GST-inclusive price by 11. On a $110 invoice the GST is $10. Taking 10% off the inclusive price gives $11, which is wrong — a mistake worth about 9% of your GST claims across a year.
When do I have to register for GST?
When your turnover reaches $75,000 in any 12-month period, or when you expect it will. Non-profits have a $150,000 threshold. Taxi and ride-share drivers must register regardless of turnover.
What is not subject to GST?
Basic food, most health and medical services, most education, childcare, and exports are GST-free. Residential rent and most financial supplies are input-taxed, which means no GST is charged and no credits can be claimed on related costs.
Can I claim GST on business purchases?
Yes, if you are registered — you claim the GST credit on purchases used in your business, provided you hold a valid tax invoice for anything over $82.50. You cannot claim credits on purchases relating to input-taxed supplies.
