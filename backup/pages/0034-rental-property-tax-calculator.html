Negative gearing is a loss, not a strategy

A negatively geared property loses money. The tax system gives you back a share of that loss at your marginal rate — at 37% you get 39c of every dollar lost, counting the levy, and you are still 61c down. It only works if capital growth exceeds the accumulated after-tax losses.

The panel above separates the two: the paper loss, and the actual cash position after the tax effect. Depreciation is the reason those differ — it reduces the tax without costing you anything in the year, which is why an investor with a depreciation schedule can be cash-flow neutral on a property that shows a loss on paper.

The three things the ATO amends most often

Interest on a redrawn loan. If you redraw against the investment loan for anything private, the interest on that portion stops being deductible. Mixed-purpose loans need apportioning, and the ATO checks.

Initial repairs. Fixing something that was already broken when you bought is capital, not a repair, however much it looks like maintenance.

Periods not available for rent. Time you or family used the property, or it was off the market, must be apportioned out. Holiday homes get particular attention.

Co-ownership

Income and deductions follow legal ownership, not who paid. A property owned 50/50 splits 50/50 regardless of whose account the mortgage comes out of. This calculator works on your full share — if you own half, enter half of everything.

Questions people actually ask

How is rental income taxed in Australia?
Rent is added to your other income and taxed at your marginal rate. Expenses incurred in earning it are deductible. If deductions exceed the rent, the loss reduces your other income — that is negative gearing.

What can I claim on an investment property?
Loan interest, council rates, water, insurance, strata levies, agent commission, advertising for tenants, repairs and maintenance, pest control, and depreciation on the building and fittings. Not the loan principal, and not improvements — those go to the cost base for CGT instead.

What is the difference between a repair and an improvement?
A repair restores something to its original condition — replacing a broken tile with the same tile. An improvement makes it better than it was — replacing a laminate kitchen with stone. Repairs are deductible now; improvements are capital works, deducted at 2.5% a year over 40 years.

Is a depreciation schedule worth it?
For most properties built after 1987, yes, comfortably. A quantity surveyor charges several hundred dollars once and typically finds thousands a year in deductions you cannot work out yourself. It is also fully deductible.

Do I pay tax when I sell?
Yes, capital gains tax on the gain, with the 50% discount if you held it over 12 months. Every capital works deduction you claimed reduces your cost base, which increases the gain — so some of the benefit is deferred rather than free.
