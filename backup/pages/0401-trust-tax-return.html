Trust tax return

A trust return is the last step of a process that starts before 30 June. The trustee has to decide who is entitled to the year’s income, record that decision in time, and report it correctly. Beneficiaries then pay tax on their share. We handle the resolution, the accounts and the return as one job.

Why the resolution matters so much

If the trustee doesn’t make a valid resolution in time, the trust’s income can end up going to the default beneficiaries named in the deed. Or the trustee can be assessed on it at the top marginal rate. Neither is usually what anyone intended, and the resolution can’t be backdated. It’s the single most expensive thing to get wrong with a discretionary trust, and it’s entirely avoidable with a reminder in June.

Distributions that don’t reach the beneficiary

Distributing income to a beneficiary on a low tax rate, while the money is actually kept or used by someone else, can fall under section 100A. The ATO has been active in this area. Distributions to adult children, and to companies, get particular attention. We look at where the money actually goes as well as who is named in the resolution.
