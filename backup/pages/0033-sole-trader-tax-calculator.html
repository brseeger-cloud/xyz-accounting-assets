The set-aside percentage is the number that matters

Employees never think about this because payroll does it for them. As a sole trader, every dollar that lands in your account contains money that is not yours. The percentage in the panel above is the share of your profit that ends up with the ATO at your current level — move it into a separate account the day each invoice is paid and the tax bill stops being an event.

If you are registered for GST, that is on top. One eleventh of every GST-inclusive payment you receive belongs to the ATO from the moment it arrives.

What you can claim that an employee cannot

  • Home office occupancy costs, where you have a dedicated work area
  • The full cost of tools and equipment used in the business, or their decline in value
  • Vehicle costs between jobs and to see clients, at 88c per kilometre or with a logbook
  • Insurance: public liability, professional indemnity, income protection
  • The cost of finding work — advertising, a website, professional subscriptions

What you cannot claim: entertainment, however business-related the conversation; your own wage, because you cannot employ yourself; and anything with a private component, unless you apportion it honestly.

The first-year trap

In your first year of business you pay no tax during the year. In your second, you pay the first year’s bill and start quarterly instalments toward the second year’s. People who put aside 25% from day one handle this comfortably. People who do not, generally do not.

Questions people actually ask

How much tax does a sole trader pay in Australia?
A sole trader is taxed at the same individual rates as an employee, on business profit rather than salary. There is no separate business rate and no tax-free amount beyond the usual $18,200 threshold. The difference is that nobody withholds it for you.

How much should I put aside for tax?
The calculator gives you the exact percentage for your profit level. As a rough guide, a sole trader earning $80,000 to $120,000 in profit should be setting aside 25% to 30% of every dollar, plus GST if registered.

When do I have to register for GST?
Once your turnover reaches $75,000 in a 12-month period, or as soon as you expect it will. Ride-share drivers must register from the first dollar. GST you collect is never your money — keep it separate.

What are PAYG instalments?
Once you have lodged a return showing business income above the ATO threshold, they put you into the instalment system so you prepay next year quarterly instead of in one lump. It is not an extra tax, just earlier timing — but the first year, when you pay last year's bill and this year's instalments together, is the one that hurts.

Should I set up a company instead?
Sometimes. A company caps the rate at 25% but adds compliance cost, and money taken out of it is taxed again in your hands. It usually starts making sense when profit consistently exceeds what you need to live on. Worth an actual conversation rather than a rule of thumb.
