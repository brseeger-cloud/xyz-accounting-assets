Why the partnership comes first

A partner’s individual return can’t be finished accurately until the partnership’s net income is settled. When the two are done by different people at different times, the shares often don’t match what the partnership lodged, and the ATO’s data matching picks that up. Doing them together avoids amendments later.

Salaries, drawings and the agreement

Partners aren’t employees of their own partnership. Money a partner takes out during the year is a drawing against their share of profit, not a wage the partnership can deduct. If your agreement provides for a “salary” to one partner, it’s treated as part of how profit is divided. Where there’s no written agreement, profit is generally split equally. That’s rarely what partners intend, and it’s the most common reason we recommend putting one in writing.

Is it really a partnership?

Two people earning income together can be a partnership for tax purposes without ever registering anything. Rental property co-owners are the usual example. They generally aren’t carrying on a business, and each owner reports their share of rent directly rather than through a partnership return. We’ll confirm which applies before you lodge a return you don’t need.
