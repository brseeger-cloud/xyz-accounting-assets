Where a refund actually comes from

Your employer withholds tax from every pay using the ATO’s tables. Those tables assume a steady income all year and no deductions at all. Almost nobody matches that assumption, which is why most people are owed money at the end of the year rather than the other way around.

Two things move the number. Deductions reduce the income you are taxed on, so the tax you owe falls and the gap between that and what was withheld grows. And events during the year — a period out of work, a job change, a pay rise late in the year — leave the withholding tables out of step with what you actually earned.

The claims most people miss

In order of how often we see them left off a return that walks in the door: working-from-home hours, the fee paid to last year’s accountant, income protection insurance premiums, union and professional association fees, and the work-use share of a phone and internet bill. None of them are exotic. They are just easy to forget twelve months later.

An estimate is not an assessment

This tool works from the figures you type in. A real return also picks up the data the ATO already holds — your income statements, bank interest, dividends, health fund details and share trades — and reconciles them. Where the two disagree, the ATO’s data wins, which is one of the main reasons a return prepared by an agent lands differently from a self-lodged estimate.

Lodging for 2025–26? The deadline if you lodge yourself is 31 October 2026. Through a registered agent it stretches to 15 May 2027, provided you are on the agent’s list before the October date. Our deadline page sets out how that works.

Questions people actually ask

How much will my tax refund be?
Your refund is the tax withheld from your pay during the year, less the tax you actually owe on your income after deductions. Enter your income statement figures above and it works out in real time. The average Australian refund sits between $2,000 and $3,000, but it depends entirely on what was withheld and what you claim.

Will claiming more deductions get me a bigger refund?
Yes — every dollar of legitimate deduction reduces your taxable income, so it comes back at your marginal rate plus the 2% Medicare levy. At the 30% bracket a $1,000 claim is worth $320. But you must have actually spent the money, it must relate to earning your income, and you need a record of it.

When will I get my refund?
The ATO usually processes an electronically lodged return within two weeks. Paper returns take up to ten weeks. Returns lodged in the first weeks of July often sit longer, because the ATO waits for employers, banks and health funds to report.

What if the calculator says I owe money instead?
That usually means not enough was withheld — most often because the tax-free threshold was claimed at two jobs, or because you have income with no withholding, like interest, dividends or a side business. It is worth talking to an agent before lodging, because there are often deductions you have not counted.

Do I need receipts for everything?
You need records for claims over $300 in total work-related expenses. Under that you still need to be able to explain how you worked the figure out. From 2026-27 the $1,000 standard deduction lets you claim without receipts at all — see our guide on it.
