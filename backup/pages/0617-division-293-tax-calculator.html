What the $250,000 actually tests

It is not your salary. The test adds your taxable income, reportable fringe benefits and any net investment or rental losses, then adds your concessional super contributions on top. Someone on $235,000 with $28,200 of employer super sits at $263,200 and pays Division 293 on the $13,200 over the line — $1,980.

The threshold has been $250,000 since 1 July 2017 and is not indexed, while the super guarantee has climbed to 12%. Each year a few more people cross it on employer contributions alone, without a pay rise.

Why super still wins at 30%

Division 293 takes the tax on your contributions from 15% to 30%. Your top marginal rate at that income is 47% including the Medicare levy. Every dollar you put into super instead of taking as pay still saves 17 cents — less than the 32 cents it saved below the threshold, but not nothing. The calculator shows the saving for your figures.

Where it changes behaviour is the negatively geared investor. The rental loss lowers your taxable income, but Division 293 adds it straight back, so gearing does not lift you out of the tax.

Paying it

The ATO issues a separate Division 293 notice after your return is assessed. You can pay it yourself within 21 days, or ask your fund to release the money using the release authority that comes with the notice. Paying from your own pocket leaves more compounding in super; releasing it is easier on cash flow. Either is fine — just do not ignore the notice, because interest runs from the due date.

Questions people actually ask

Is Division 293 tax charged on all my super contributions?
Only on concessional contributions — employer super, salary sacrifice and personal contributions you claim a deduction for — and only on the lesser of those contributions or the amount by which your combined income and contributions exceed $250,000. After-tax contributions are never touched.
My income is under $250,000. Why did I get a Division 293 notice?
Because the test adds your super contributions to your income. On $225,000 with $27,000 of employer super you are at $252,000 and owe $300. Reportable fringe benefits and investment losses are added too, so a company car or a negatively geared property can tip you over.
Should I stop salary sacrificing if I have to pay it?
Usually not. Contributions caught by Division 293 are taxed at 30% in total, which is still well below the 47% you would pay taking the same money as salary. The concession is smaller, not gone. The exceptions are people who need the cash now, or who are already at their concessional cap.
Do excess concessional contributions attract Division 293 tax?
No. Contributions above your cap are excess contributions — they are added to your taxable income and taxed at your marginal rate, with a 15% offset for the tax the fund already paid. Division 293 applies only to contributions within the cap, including any carry-forward cap you are using.
Can I pay it from my super?
Yes. The notice comes with a release authority you can send to your fund, which pays the ATO from your balance. You have 60 days to do that, or 21 days to pay it yourself. Members of defined benefit funds can defer the debt until a benefit is paid, with interest.
