The trade, in one line

You give up income taxed at your marginal rate and receive it in super taxed at 15%. The whole benefit is the gap between those two rates, so it grows with your income and disappears near the bottom of the scale.

At the 30% bracket the gap is 17c in the dollar once the Medicare levy is counted. At 45% it is 32c. At 15% or 16% there is essentially nothing in it, and the money is locked away for decades — which is a real cost, not a footnote.

The cap includes your employer’s contributions

This is where people get caught. On a $150,000 salary, employer super at 12% is already $18,000 of the $30,000 cap. Sacrificing another $15,000 puts you over, and the excess is taxed at your marginal rate anyway plus an interest charge. The panel above tracks the cap as you type.

If your total super balance was under $500,000 at the previous 30 June, you can use unused cap from the past five years — which is how someone with a windfall or a capital gain can make a large contribution in a single year without breaching anything.

Before you set it up

A sacrifice arrangement must be agreed with your employer before you earn the income; you cannot apply it retrospectively to money you have already been paid. Check that your employer calculates its super guarantee on your pre-sacrifice salary — since 2020 it must, but payroll systems still get it wrong.

Questions people actually ask

How does salary sacrificing into super save tax?
Money sacrificed is taken before tax, so instead of being taxed at your marginal rate it is taxed at 15% going into super. If your marginal rate is 30% plus the 2% levy, you save 17c in the dollar.

How much can I salary sacrifice?
The concessional cap is $30,000 a year and it includes your employer's contributions, not just yours. Going over means the excess is taxed at your marginal rate, which removes the benefit entirely — unless you have unused cap carried forward from the last five years.

Is salary sacrifice worth it on a low income?
Less so. If your marginal rate is 15% or 16%, the 15% contributions tax leaves almost nothing on the table, and the money is locked up until preservation age. Below about $45,000 the government co-contribution for after-tax contributions is usually the better route.

Does salary sacrifice reduce my HECS repayment?
No. Sacrificed super is added back when the ATO works out your repayment income, so your compulsory HECS repayment is unchanged. It is one of the more common misconceptions.

Can I sacrifice things other than super?
Yes — a novated car lease, a work laptop, professional memberships. Non-super sacrifice generally creates a reportable fringe benefit, which counts towards the Medicare levy surcharge, HECS repayments and family assistance tests even though it is not taxable income.
