Superannuation refund

If you worked in Australia on a temporary visa and have since left, you can generally claim your super back as a departing Australia superannuation payment (DASP). If you’re staying, you may have lost or unclaimed super sitting with the ATO or an old fund. We help with both, including the final tax return that usually belongs alongside a DASP claim.

Who can claim a DASP

  • You worked in Australia on an eligible temporary visa
  • You have left Australia
  • Your visa has expired or been cancelled
  • You are not an Australian or New Zealand citizen, or an Australian permanent resident

You can’t claim while you’re still in Australia on the visa. Holders of some visa types, and people who later become permanent residents, aren’t eligible.

Why the tax return matters

A DASP is final. The tax withheld from it can’t be claimed back in a tax return. But the tax withheld from your wages during your last year in Australia can be. If you left partway through the year, you may be owed a refund on your pay, and that return is separate from the super claim. We handle both so neither is missed.

Lost and unclaimed super

If you’ve changed jobs, moved house or had several funds, some of your super may be in an account you’ve forgotten. Or it may have been transferred to the ATO as unclaimed money. You can see every account linked to your TFN in myGov. Where you’re under preservation age, the usual answer is to consolidate it into your current fund rather than withdraw it. Early access is only allowed on limited grounds, and anyone offering to release your super early for a fee is almost certainly running a scam.

Excess contributions

If you went over a contribution cap, the ATO may issue a determination giving you the option to release the excess from your fund. The time limits on responding are short. If you’ve received one of those letters, send it to us straight away.
