What counts as a disposal

  • Selling crypto for Australian dollars
  • Swapping one cryptocurrency for another, including stablecoins
  • Using crypto to buy goods or services
  • Gifting it to someone

Transferring between your own wallets is not a disposal. Neither is buying and holding. But every one of the four above needs an Australian dollar value recorded at the moment it happened, which is why exchange records matter more in crypto than in almost any other asset class.

Parcels, not balances

CGT applies to each parcel you bought, not to your holding as a whole. If you bought Bitcoin three times at different prices and sold part of it, you need to identify which parcel you sold. You can choose the method, but you must apply it consistently and be able to show your working. This calculator handles one parcel at a time — for a year of trading, an agent working from your exchange export is faster and considerably safer.

Investor or trader

An investor holds crypto for capital growth and pays CGT with access to the 50% discount. A trader carries on a business of trading, treats crypto as trading stock, pays tax on the full profit as ordinary income, and can deduct losses against other income. Which one you are is a question of fact, not a choice — frequency, volume, intention and business-like organisation all count. Most people are investors, and the ATO will say so if you have claimed otherwise without the substance to back it.

Questions people actually ask

Do I pay tax on crypto in Australia?
Yes. The ATO treats crypto as property, not currency, so disposing of it is a CGT event. Selling for dollars, swapping one coin for another, spending it on goods and gifting it are all disposals. Simply holding it is not.

Is swapping one coin for another taxable?
Yes, and it is the most commonly missed event. Swapping BTC for ETH is a disposal of the BTC at its Australian dollar value at that moment, whether or not any dollars change hands.

Does the ATO know about my crypto?
Almost certainly. Australian exchanges report to the ATO under the data-matching program, covering millions of accounts. Unreported disposals are one of the most common triggers for a review.

What if I made a loss?
A crypto loss is a capital loss. It cannot reduce your salary income, but it offsets capital gains this year and carries forward indefinitely against future gains. It is worth recording even in a year where you have no gains.

Do I get the 50% discount on crypto?
Yes, if you held the particular parcel more than 12 months and you are an individual holding as an investor. Crypto held as trading stock by a genuine trading business is treated as ordinary income instead, with no discount.
