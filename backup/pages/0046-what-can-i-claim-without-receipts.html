What can I claim on tax without receipts?

You can claim up to $300 of work-related expenses without written evidence, plus laundry to $150, car use to 5,000 km and home-office hours at the fixed rate. From 2026-27 a $1,000 standard deduction applies with no receipts at all.

Updated 21 August 2026 Current for 2025-26, 2026-27

The honest answer is more generous than most people expect and less generous than the internet suggests.

The $300 rule, precisely

If your total work-related expenses come to $300 or less, you can claim them without written evidence. You still have to have spent the money, and you still have to be able to explain how you arrived at the figure if asked.

The detail that catches people: it is a cliff, not a threshold. Claim

$301 and you need written evidence for all of it, not just the dollar over. There is no $300 free allowance sitting underneath a larger claim.

Some categories sit outside the $300 total — car expenses, meal allowances, award transport payments and travel allowances are counted separately and have their own rules.

Claims that never need receipts

Laundry, to $150. At $1 per load for work-only clothing, or $0.50 where work clothes are washed with personal ones. Under $150 you need no receipts, though you do need a reasonable basis for the number of loads.

Car expenses, to 5,000 km. At 88c per kilometre you can claim without a logbook — but you need to be able to show how you worked out the kilometres, for instance a diary of a representative period. It is not a free

$4,400 and the ATO writes to people who claim exactly 5,000 km with nothing behind it.

Working from home at the fixed rate. No receipts for the individual bills, but you need a record of the actual hours worked at home across the whole year. Since 2022-23 an estimate or a four-week sample is not accepted.

Small amounts under $10, to a total of

$200, where getting a receipt was not reasonably possible.

From 2026-27: the $1,000 standard deduction

This is the significant change. For the 2026-27 year onwards, ITAA 1997 s 25-130 gives every taxpayer a $1,000 standard deduction for work-related expenses with no receipts and no spending required at all.

It replaces your itemised claim rather than adding to it — you get whichever is higher. For anyone whose genuine work expenses come in under

$1,000, the receipt question largely goes away.

Note the year. The return you are lodging now, for the year ended 30 June 2026, is 2025-26 and the old rules apply to it.

What “no receipts” does not mean

It does not mean you can claim things you did not pay for. Every rule above is a substantiation concession — a relaxation of the evidence you must keep, not of the requirement that the expense was real, work-related and yours.

The ATO’s data-matching now covers bank accounts, share registries, crypto exchanges, health funds and property transactions. Claims that look statistically odd for an occupation and income get letters. A deduction you cannot explain is worth considerably less than the tax it saves.

What to do instead of keeping receipts

Photograph them. The ATO accepts a photo of a receipt as written evidence, and the myDeductions tool in the ATO app records the expense, the date and the amount for you and pushes it into your return. Five seconds at the counter removes the whole problem.
