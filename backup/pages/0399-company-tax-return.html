When a company return is due

Company due dates depend on the company’s size and lodgement history. When a registered agent lodges, most small companies fall due later than they would if the directors lodged themselves. New companies and companies with overdue returns can have earlier dates, so we confirm yours at the start rather than assume.

The issue we find most often

Money taken out of a company isn’t the director’s money until it’s paid as wages, a dividend or a properly documented loan. If a director draws funds and the loan isn’t repaid or put under a complying loan agreement by the time the return is lodged, Division 7A can treat the amount as an unfranked dividend. That can mean tax at the director’s marginal rate on money they may already have spent. We check the loan account every year before lodging, while it can still be fixed.

Dormant and loss-making companies

A company that didn’t trade still has to lodge, or tell the ATO a return isn’t necessary in a way that matches its circumstances. Losses are worth recording properly: whether they can be used in a later year depends on the company passing the continuity of ownership or business continuity tests. ASIC’s annual review fee is separate from the tax return and payable even when the company is dormant.
