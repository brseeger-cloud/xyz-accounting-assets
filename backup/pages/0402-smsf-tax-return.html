Before it can be lodged, the fund’s accounts must be audited by an approved SMSF auditor who is independent of the fund and of whoever prepared the accounts. How an SMSF is taxed

A complying fund generally pays 15% tax on its assessable income, including concessional contributions and earnings, with a one-third discount on capital gains on assets held for more than 12 months. Earnings on assets supporting retirement-phase pensions are generally exempt, which is why pension reporting has to be right. Some income, such as non-arm’s length income, is taxed at the top rate.

When the return is due

A newly registered fund’s first return has its own due date, which is earlier than later years. After that, due dates depend on the fund’s lodgement history. Funds lodging through a registered agent usually have more time. The audit has to be finished before lodgement, so we don’t leave it to the last month.

The audit isn’t a formality

The auditor checks the accounts and whether the trustees followed super law. Typical issues are loans to members, assets not held in the fund’s name, and investments that don’t meet the sole purpose test. The auditor must report serious breaches to the ATO. We’d rather find those issues while preparing the accounts, when they can often still be fixed.
